CHAPTER 1: WELCOME TO GREYWICK!

The moment I woke up, Greywick was exactly how I remembered it. I had only moved here yesterday, but everything had been going pretty well so far. I got out of bed and headed to the kitchen, where I made myself some breakfast. After eating, I wandered around the house for a few minutes, trying to figure out what to do with the rest of my morning. Eventually, I decided to continue coding the game I'd been working on. After spending some time on it, I went outside to water my plants and, as I liked to call it, touch some grass.

Greywick was a fairly small town, with rows of old-fashioned houses lining the streets and a handful of shops and businesses scattered around the center. Most of the buildings looked like they had been there for decades, but they were still well maintained. There was a grocery store, a bank, a school, a library, a gas station, and even an old train station that I hadn't gotten around to visiting yet. The town was quiet, but not in a bad way. It just felt like the kind of place where everyone knew everyone. While I was looking around, the sky suddenly darkened, and rain began pouring down harder than I expected. I stepped closer to the window and noticed a man walking down the street. He wasn't running, covering himself, or even looking bothered by the rain. He simply walked at the same steady pace, as if the storm wasn't happening at all. I watched him for a few seconds before shrugging and turning away. Maybe he just really liked rain. Anyway, Greywick had a lot more to it than I had originally expected.

A few hours had passed since the rain started, and by then it had finally begun to calm down. The streets were still wet, with puddles scattered across the sidewalks and water dripping from the roofs of the houses. I decided to take a walk to Greywick Market since I needed a few things anyway. As I walked down the street, I noticed one of the neighbors standing outside his house. He looked around my age, though I wasn't completely sure. He noticed me walking past and gave me a small wave.

“New around here?” he asked.

“Yeah,” I replied. “Moved in yesterday.”

He smiled. “Welcome to Greywick.”

“Thanks.”

He nodded and went back inside without saying anything else. I continued toward the market, barely thinking about the interaction. It was probably just another normal day in Greywick.

After a few more minutes of walking, I finally reached Greywick Market. The little bell above the entrance jingled as I pushed the door open, and I was greeted by the smell of fresh bread and the quiet hum of people talking. The market wasn't very large, but it had pretty much everything I needed, from food and drinks to household supplies. I grabbed a basket and started walking through the aisles, passing a few people along the way. An older woman near the produce section smiled at me as I walked by.

“Are you the new kid?” she asked.

“Yeah,” I said. “I moved here yesterday.”

“Silas, right?”

I stopped for a second. “Uh... yeah.”

She smiled again. “Thought so. Welcome to Greywick.”

I thanked her and continued down the aisle, slightly confused about how she knew my name. I figured someone must have mentioned it to her. Besides, I had more important things to worry about, like figuring out what kind of cereal Greywick Market had.

After grabbing a box of cereal and a few other things, I paid for everything and left the market. Instead of taking the same road home, I decided to take a shortcut through the older part of town. I hadn't really explored that area yet, and I figured I might as well see what was there. Most of the houses looked older than the ones near my neighborhood, and the streets were much quieter. As I walked, I noticed a large, worn-down building sitting behind a row of trees. It looked like it had been abandoned for years, with boarded-up windows and vines covering parts of the walls. I slowed down and stared at it for a moment. There wasn't anything particularly interesting about the building, but something about it made me curious. I checked the street sign and realized I had never even heard of this road before. “Huh,” I muttered, looking toward the building. “Guess I found somewhere new.”

I walked a little closer to the building, mostly because I was curious. The front door was boarded up, so there wasn't much to see from the outside. I stepped onto the old porch and looked through one of the gaps in the boards covering the windows. Inside, I could barely make out an empty hallway covered in dust. There were a few old pieces of furniture scattered around, but nothing that seemed particularly interesting. I was about to leave when I noticed something strange. At the end of the hallway was a door that looked completely clean, almost as if it had been placed there recently. I stared at it for a few seconds before looking away and then back again. The door was still there, but now it looked just as dusty as everything else. I blinked and rubbed my eyes. “Guess the light was playing tricks on me,” I muttered. I stepped off the porch and continued walking home.

I kept walking home, trying not to think about the building anymore. The rain had stopped completely, and the streets were starting to dry as the clouds slowly moved away. By the time I reached my house, the whole thing already felt like a distant thought. I put the groceries away, grabbed my laptop, and went back to working on my game. Everything was normal again. The quiet clicking of my keyboard filled the room as I worked, and for a while, I completely forgot about the strange door I'd seen. Whatever it was, it probably wasn't important.

It was getting late, so I decided to close my laptop and get some fresh air before heading upstairs. I stepped outside and looked down the street. Everything was quiet, and the last bit of sunlight was disappearing behind the clouds. For some reason, I glanced back toward the old building I'd passed earlier. I stopped. Something looked different. I stared at the building for a few seconds, trying to figure out what had caught my attention. Then I realized what it was. One of the boards covering the front windows was gone. I could see the dark hallway inside again, but the clean door I'd noticed earlier was nowhere to be seen. I looked closer at the entrance. There was only a boarded-up doorway. “I... I could've sworn there was a door boarded up here...” I said quietly. I stood there for another moment before shaking my head. Maybe I was just tired. I went back inside and shut the door behind me, leaving the old building alone in the dark.
